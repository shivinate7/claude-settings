# A brief's claim about main carries the probe that showed it

## The ruling

Rule `brief-claim-has-probe` in `hooks/guard.py` refuses an Agent or Task brief to a builder or a test-author that claims what main passes or refuses for an input. Main here is main, origin/main or "today". The verbs are passes, allows, accepts, refuses, rejects and blocks. The brief passes when it carries the probe that showed the claim. Its home is `brief_claim_gaps` in `hooks/guard.py`, beside the other brief checks (`marker_scan`, `builder_third_round`). Layer: the coordinator's order, 2026-11-07 CST, after the incident below.

- The mistake (2026-11-07 16:08 CST, PR #1581): a test-author brief said main passes `globalHotkey: Ctrl+Alt+2` and "password: none for guests and visitors". Main refused all of them. The brief was written from memory of main. The builder narrowed the rule to match, and the narrowing let through secrets that main refuses. A fix check caught it before merge.
- The harm: an agent builds to a false picture of main. The error is shipped as a test or a rule, and it looks reviewed because the brief said so.
- An input: a backtick span, a double-quoted string, or a single-quoted string that opens after a non-word character. A double-quoted string that holds backticks is one quoted data span, so a quoted case text such as `"main refuses 'X'"` written with backticks is data, not a claim. A lone quote or an apostrophe (`Don't`) opens nothing. A lone backtick reads to the line's end.
- A claim, active form: `main` or `origin/main` (a backticked `main` is still the word) with the verb next to it, or one adverb between (still, also, now, today, currently, already, even, always, never). Or "today" followed by it, they, guard, hook, rule or check, then the verb. They is read only with an -ed or negated verb. "The main session" and "the main checkout" have no verb next to main, so they are no subject. "Today the builder passes ..." names no subject from that list, so it is a time word. The inputs are those after the verb. With none there, every input in the clause counts only when nothing but today, now or punctuation follows the verb ("For `x` main refuses."); "Edit `a`, because main passes CI." claims nothing about `a`. A pronoun tail is the exception: with it or them after the verb, the clause's own inputs count ("Edit `x`, because main refuses it."). A subject (it, they, guard, hook, rule, check, with an optional the or this) may come first, with "today" later in the sentence: "The guard refuses `x` today."
- A claim, passive form: `X` is refused on main, `X` refused by main, `X` passes today, `a` and `b` pass on main, "listed `X` as passing on main". The verb may be a base form (pass, allow, accept, refuse, reject, block) or an -ing form. The inputs are those before the verb.
- A claim, framed form: a leading "On main," or "Today", then the inputs, then the verb ("On main, `a` passes.", "Today `a` and `b` pass."). The inputs are those between the frame and the verb. "Today the builder passes `x`" is no frame: a word that is no input stands between.
- Verbs: passes, allows, accepts, refuses, rejects, blocks, and their -ed forms. A negated verb counts: `do`, `does` or `did` with `not` or `n't` before pass, allow, accept, refuse, reject or block. A passive negation counts too, as in `x isn't refused on main` and `x won't pass on main`, with `x` as the input. So does `lets X through`, and `doesn't let X through`.
- Clause scope: a claim clause ends at ", so", ", and" or ", then". "origin/main passes CI, so run `verdict pytest` first" claims nothing about `verdict pytest`.
- A claim split from its inputs: a claim clause with no input of its own may say it or them. It may also say both, all, each or these at the clause end, or before "of them", "of these" or "of those". Then it takes the inputs of its own sentence if it has any. Else it takes those of the sentence right before it, or of the whole run of list lines right before it. "main passes all tests" and "main refuses each push" have no referent. A count word after both, all, each or these is a referent too: one, two to ten, a number, case, cases, input or inputs. Today or now may follow it ("Main refuses all three.", "both cases", "these two", "each one"). Both, all, each or these followed by today or now alone is a referent too ("Main refuses both today."). A claim clause with no input that ends with `:` takes the inputs of the list lines after it. It stops at the first non-blank line that is not bulleted or numbered. A blank line or a paired fence marker does not stop it. It also stops at a bulleted or numbered line with no input that begins with "Controls" and ends with `:` ("Controls that must still pass:"). The lines under that heading are not claimed.
- A NUL character in a brief is read as a space, so a literal `\x00` pair cannot pose as an input marker and crash the scan.
- Fenced text is read for claims. A claim in a fence after `Cases:` counts.
- A path is a locator, not an input, when it follows in, at, from, under, inside, within or `via` and has no space and holds `/` or `.`.
- A `Must deny:` or `Add a case:` line is read like any other line. Its case text is data when it is double-quoted (see input). An unquoted claim on it, such as `(main allows 'x: y' today)` with backticks, is read.
- A claim sentence with no input names nothing to probe and is not judged.
- A probe for an input: a command line that holds the input as the whole text between quotes, or as the whole line, with a result line right under it. A longer input that merely contains it (`git push --force` for `git push`) is another input. The command line stands on a line of its own, in a fence, in backticks, or after `$ ` or `> `. It starts with python, python3, git, verdict, bash, sh, node, npm, npx, pytest, gh, grep, rg or jq. Any number of these prefixes may come first: `cd <dir> &&`, `echo ... |`, `NAME=value`, `time`. The result line is the next non-blank line, or text after the closing backtick on the same line. The input may stand in the result line the same way. A line that holds a claim is a claim first, so a claim line is never its own probe. A command with no result line, or a result line with no command, is no probe.
- Scope: the roles `builder` and `test-author`, on an Agent or Task call. A reviewer, Explore, designer or any other role passes. A Workflow `agent()` call is not read.
- The scan is one pass over the lines. A claim on any line, line 30 and later too, is read.
- The refusal names every input with no probe, and the remedy: add the command and its result, or drop the claim. It names no forbidden target.
- The override lifts it (`owner-override`). It is not in `never_lifted`: the owner may mean the brief as it stands.
- Accepted limits: a claim in other verbs (`denies`, `fails`) or other words (`on the main branch`) is not read. An active `won't` (`main won't refuse x`) and a passive "let through" are not read. A referent looks back one sentence or one run of list lines, never further, and not past a clause break in its own sentence ("Fix 'X', and main refuses it." names nothing). A list of three joined by ", and" loses its last item to the clause break. A claim written inside a double-quoted string that holds backticks is data. A stray double quote (an inch mark) can open such a span. Only the four probe prefixes above are read. A command that reads its input from a file, with no quotes around the input and no result line that quotes it, is no probe: put the input on the command line in quotes. No case pins that a referent after a list takes every list line, not only the last one. A goal in a base verb form ("make `x` pass on main") reads as a claim. No case pins "of these" or "of those" as a referent; "of them" is read through "them". No case pins that today after the verb still lets the active form take the clause's inputs. A bulleted heading other than "Controls" does not end a claimed list, and no case pins that. A subject-first claim with "today" in a later clause of the sentence reads as a claim. A count phrase with other words ("both of the cases", "all the cases") is not read. These are forms Claude is unlikely to write by mistake (`guard-guards-mistakes-only`).
  - Any "it" or "them" after the verb counts as the referent, even one that means something else. So "Edit `x` because main passes the test it covers." fires. So does "Edit `x`. Main passes CI, it is green.", and the head of PR #1664 fires on it too.
  - A count word followed by a word other than today or now is not read: "all three cases", "all four inputs", "these two inputs", "all 12 cases". These fired on the head of PR #1664 and no longer fire.
  - One claim per sentence is read. An active claim wins over an earlier passive or framed one, so "Today `a` passes, and main refuses `b`." names only `b`. Of two active claims the first wins: "main passes `x`, and main refuses `y`" names only `x`, as on the head of PR #1664. For "The guard refuses `a` today, and main refuses `b`." the head of PR #1664 names `b` and this rule names `a`.
  - A goal in a base or -ing verb form reads as a claim: "`verdict pytest` must still pass on main", "Keep `x` passing on main", "Check that `x` and `y` pass on main". None of 55 real briefs hit these.
  - A subject-first "lets ... today ... through", with "through" after the last "today", is not read, as on the head of PR #1664. That form appears in 74 of 200k random sentences and in no real brief.
- Cost: pure text work inside the guard call already running. No process start, no file written, no log line beyond the refusal's own. The subject-first form is searched only up to the sentence's last "today", so 1500 repeats of "it refuses" on one line take 0.002 to 0.008 s, measured.

Protected by the `brief-claim:` cases in `hooks/test_guard.py`: deny and allow cases, plus one log check. They were red before the rule landed. Run `MUTATE_ONLY="brief-claim:" python3 hooks/test_guard.py` for the case count; its final line reports the cases that ran. The mutants are the `brief-claim:` entries in `hooks/mutate_guard.py`. Each goes red on one named case. An edge no case pins has no mutant. Many edges are unpinned. Examples: the -ed verbs but refused, the adverbs but never, the locator words but in, and the probe commands but python3 and git.
