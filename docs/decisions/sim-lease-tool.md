# bin/sim-lease is the one simulator queue and lease on a Mac

## The rulings

One tool, `bin/sim-lease`, holds the Mac-wide queue and lease for phone simulators. It replaces the Mac sim gatekeeper session as the thing that grants a boot. The gatekeeper (a session) reads `sim-lease status` and need not stay awake. The mistake it stops: a session boots a phone simulator outside the gatekeeper's one queue, or on a busy machine.

1. Owner rulings, degen `shared-sim-lease`, 2026-11-02 13:49 to 14:47 CST, as `skills/ios-apps/SKILL.md` ("Sharing the Mac") cites them. The next in line takes the first slot to free. The owner then ruled (`sims-load-brake-only`, owner 2026-11-12 21:59 CST): "No hard-coded simulator limit or pairing. Run as many simulators as the work needs, limited only by the load brake." That replaced the rules that one phone boots at a time, that the 18 Pro never runs beside a Duo, and that slot 2 needs slot 1 in use. Ruling 3 names the limits that still hold beyond the brake, as a known gap.
2. `sim-lease take --device duo|pro --pid PID [--udid]` joins one strict FIFO queue and waits for its turn. Only the head is granted. It prints the slot (`1`, `2` or `pro`), or with `--udid` that slot's device UDID from `SLOT_UDID`, and exits 0. At the wait limit (`SIM_LEASE_WAIT`, default 300 s) it exits 1 with one stderr line that names the holders, the brake's readings and the busy ones. `--pid` is required on `take` and `give`; a usage error, a missing `--pid` included, prints one usage line on stderr and exits 2.
3. Limits on every grant: the machine is not busy by the machine-load brake's one measure (owner, 2026-11-12, Cockpit item 554 pick a; decision `machine-load-brake`). Busy is memory over 85%, or disk free under the smaller of 10% of the disk and 15 GB. The tool calls `read_load` and `load_busy` in `hooks/guard.py`, loaded by path, and keeps no bar of its own. The bar covers every slot, so a busy machine grants none. A reading that cannot be read (memory unread, disk unreadable) counts as normal and grants, while the brake is loaded. The load average is never read: it was 107 on 15 cores while `top` showed half idle. A duo take gets the first free of slots 1 and 2, and a pro take gets slot `pro`. No rule pairs devices or caps their count (`sims-load-brake-only`, owner 2026-11-12 21:59 CST). The brake is the only rule that refuses on load. The queue stays strict FIFO across devices, so a free pro waits behind a queued duo head.
   Known gap: the tool still limits simulators beyond the load brake. `SLOT_UDID` lists three known devices, so a take for a third Duo or a second 18 Pro at once waits for a slot to free. Strict FIFO holds a free device behind the queue's head. The Mac lock in `bin/sim-test` runs one `sim-test` at a time. `sims-load-brake-only` settles the direction: a queue and a lock per device, then devices made on demand, limited by the load brake only. Step `step:sims-on-demand` (Simulators come on demand, limited only by the load brake) holds that work.
4. `bin/sim-test` takes the lease for its role (`--device duo` by default, else `pro`; it boots the UDID from `take --udid`) on its own-boot path only, after its lock and the reuse check and before `simctl boot`. It gives the lease back after the shutdown and before it frees its lock, on every exit, INT and TERM too. The reuse path takes no lease. A `--hold` run keeps its lease for the whole hold.
5. Recovery does not depend on the holder ending well (`verification-recovery-independent-of-state`). Each call reaps a lease or a waiter whose pid is dead, or whose `ps -o lstart=` start time differs from the one recorded at take (a reused pid). `ps` runs with `TZ=UTC`, so a start time does not change with the caller's `TZ`. A holder that dies never has to give.
6. State: `<config>/state/sim-lease/leases.json`, under `fcntl.flock` on `lock` in the same folder, written through a temp file and `os.replace`. `<config>` is `$CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`, else `~/.claude`.
7. No-op: when `uname -s` is not Darwin, or `CI` or `GITHUB_ACTIONS` equals `true`, `take` prints `none` (with `--udid` too) and exits 0, `give` exits 0, and no file is written. On Darwin, a no-op `take` also prints one stderr line saying no lease was taken. Any other value, `false` or empty, is not a no-op. So CI runs and Linux hosts are never queued.
8. Brake not loaded (decision `guard-hooks-fail-closed`, a guard with a missing part refuses the call): when `hooks/guard.py` or one of its family modules cannot be loaded, the brake is absent, and an absent brake grants nothing. `take` exits 1 with one stderr line that says "brake could not be loaded", leases nothing and leaves no queue place. `status` prints "brake could not be loaded" on stdout and exits 0. The tool adds no retry of its own. `hooks/guard.py` waits about 5 s for a missing part before it raises. A broken family module therefore adds that wait to each `take`. The failed load also writes one `guard-crash` line to `guard.log`, through the guard's own writer.
9. Cost: per process, `hooks/guard.py` and its family modules load once, about 0.2 s (measured here, one machine), and `lint/test_sim_lease.py` pins that the load is read. Then each call, and each poll of a waiting take, makes at most one memory read and one disk read in the tool's own process. It starts no `sysctl` and no child process for the load, as `lint/test_sim_lease.py` pins (cases Brake, MemoryBrake, BrokenBrake). Each call runs at most one `uname` and one `ps` per recorded pid, and a waiting take repeats the `ps` reads every `SIM_LEASE_POLL` seconds (default 2). It writes `leases.json` only when the content changes, plus the empty `lock` file (unmeasured: no test checks a rewrite). No daemon, no timer, no log of its own.

Test: `lint/test_sim_lease.py` (slots, the busy bar at its edges in class `Brake`, the memory bar in `MemoryBrake`, a brake that cannot load in `BrokenBrake`, no `sysctl` call and no child but `uname` and `ps` in `test_a_take_and_a_status_never_call_sysctl` and `test_a_take_starts_no_child_process_but_uname_and_ps`, a high load average that grants, queue order, reaping by dead pid and by changed start time, give, usage, no-op, concurrent takes) and cases 47 to 50 of `lint/test_sim_test.sh` (lease held at boot, released after the run and after TERM, a busy disk boots nothing and `sysctl` is not called, reuse path takes none). Both use shims for `uname` and `ps` and the fake disk in `tests/sim_lease/sitecustomize.py`; the brake's own bar is pinned in family `load-brake` of `hooks/test_guard.py` (the `load_busy` cases).

Not proven here: there is no Mac in the cloud. The Mac memory counter (`kern.memorystatus_level`), the real `ps -o lstart=` format on macOS and a real `simctl` boot under the lease are untested here. The test seam (`tests/sim_lease/sitecustomize.py`) reads memory from `SIM_LEASE_TEST_MEMORY`, so `MemoryBrake` pins the memory bar with set readings; the real counter is not read here. `hooks/test_guard_load_os.py` checks the memory reading on the Mac runner. The shell-macos CI job runs the sim-test suite on a Mac, but with a fake disk and a shimmed `ps`.

Not covered: a SIGKILL of `sim-test` skips its EXIT trap, so its simulator (a Duo or the 18 Pro) stays booted while its lease is reaped. The next `sim-test` run reads the booted list after its grant: the same role reuses the device (debt note `sim-test-sigkill-leaves-sim-booted`). An `xcodebuild -destination` boot skips the lease, and the route rule refuses it (decision `sim-test-is-the-one-boot-path`, ruling 5). A simulator MCP `launch` of a shut-down device is allowed only to a session that holds a live lease for that device. The guard reads `leases.json` through `live_leases` and `SLOT_UDID` in `bin/sim-lease`.
