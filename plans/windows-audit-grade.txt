**Whole-repo grade: C+.** Four weeks old (first commit 2026-09-14), 1,383 commits, 327 merged PRs, 52k lines of code of which 60% are tests, 101 rules, 79 decision records. The machinery is serious and mostly honest about its limits. It is not yet pointed at the two outcomes: the repo measures its code well and its outcomes almost never. The one log that does measure outcomes, `~/.claude/guard.log`, nobody reads.

## The number that frames everything

`hooks/guard.py:4401-4412` appends every verdict to `~/.claude/guard.log`. 23 days, 9,537 lines: 5,508 noted, 3,837 deny, 174 ask. On 2026-10-10 and 10-11: 594 and 523 denies per day. Each deny is one agent turn thrown away, then a retry.

Top deny rules: `waiter` 1,087, `silent-write` 920, `env-file` 558, `detached-launch` 296, `test-author-scope` 237, `shared-tree` 205, `machine-wide-kill` 135. `waiter` alone is 28% of all denies: agents reach for `sleep`, the guard refuses, and `settings.json` also denies the `Monitor` tool, so an agent that must wait on a condition has no allowed way to do it. The repo's two wait rules collide with each other and cost about 47 denies per day.

The asks: 94 `shared-tree`, 58 `force-push`, 22 `subagent-model-cap` in 23 days. Only 42 of the 94 are `git checkout`/`restore`, and the last 8 days hold 9 asks in total. So the worktree-discard branch's "prompt frequency is unmeasured" is wrong: it is measured, in this log, at about 1 to 2 per day. The record's claim that "an ask leaves no line" names the wrong file.

## Area 1: review rounds. Grade C

- 327 merged PRs: median 2 commits, mean 4.9, p90 12, max 76. 39 PRs (12%) carry 10 or more commits; 12 carry 20 or more. Median open-to-merge 0.5 h, p90 2.6 h. GitHub reviews: 0. GitHub comments: 4. Every review happens inside agent lanes, so the PR record cannot show rounds; only PR bodies and records do (`decisions/rm-on-a-variable-needs-a-guard.md:39`: "seven review rounds"; PR #340 body: six; #342: three; #338: two).
- Why rounds pile up: the long PRs are all shell or script scanners in `hooks/guard.py` (6,443 lines, 71 rule names). A reviewer told to "hunt bypasses" (CLAUDE.md `roles-each-layer-proves-once`) against a regex-and-tokenizer model of bash will always find one more form. The day's three largest PRs each ended by writing the remaining forms as "Named limits" (12 such bullets across records) and merging. That is the right end state; it arrives three rounds late.
- `agents/reviewer.md` and `agents/test-author.md` run on `haiku`; `agents/builder.md` names no model. Whether the model drives the round count: unmeasured.
- A round takes about one full CI wall clock (8 min) plus the lane. The cost is agent time and owner attention, not GitHub minutes (billable 0: public repo).

Fix shape: a round cap, not a better reviewer. Two rounds; after that every open bypass becomes a named limit in the record and the PR merges. The repo already does this by instinct; make it the rule and the reviewer brief.

## Area 3: testing. Grade B- for code, D for outcomes

- `hooks/test_guard.py`: 7,552 lines, about 1,219 verdict tuples, one process; 173 s on Linux, 122 s on macOS, 330 s on Windows. `hooks/mutate_guard.py` (1,527 lines, about 288 anchors) runs the whole suite per mutant: 176 s. `janitor/mutate_sweep.py` (1,318 lines, about 131 anchors) exists because, per its own docstring, "the builder mutated 22 assertions BY HAND, reported 22 kills, and committed nothing". That is a real incident and a right fix.
- Red-then-green is practiced: the day's PR bodies cite red commits, and the test-author writes first. The "WRONG CAUSE" verdict in the harnesses (a kill must name the case the mutant claims to break) is above-average rigor.
- Ratio: 31.8k test lines to 20.1k product lines. For a guard that fires 3,837 times in 23 days, that ratio is defensible. For 7,552 lines in one file with no family split, it is a 3 to 5 minute tax on every guard change, and the mutation harness multiplies it.
- What the tests prove: that `guard.py` returns the fixture's verdict, and that the janitor stops a fixture process. What they do not prove: that the owner saw fewer prompts, that a false deny rate fell, that two sessions did not collide. The telemetry for those exists and is unread: `guard.log` (asks and denies by rule), `state/agent-end-payloads.jsonl` (199 lines), `state/janitor-runs.jsonl` (44 lines). No check in `lint/` or CI reads any of them. `decisions/` says "unmeasured" 43 times.
