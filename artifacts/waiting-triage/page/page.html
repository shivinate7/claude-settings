Mockup 1 of 3: decision-first

Why the page looks like a triage inbox

You open this page after an overnight run with a pile of items. The job is to clear the pile fast, starting with whatever is holding work up, and to know each answer landed. Every choice below serves that job. The sample items are the pilot's real items from Oct 26, with urgency and "what it blocks" added by hand to show the new fields.

The choices

  • Sorted by what it blocks, not by number. Each item says what is stuck until you answer ("every batch waits for the Mac"). Items that hold up work sit at the top. The number stays, so "5 a" in the thread still works.
  • One item at a time, with the queue beside it. The queue gives you the whole pile at a glance. The right pane gives one question your full attention. Long context folds to three lines, so the options are on screen without scrolling.
  • Options side by side. Each option is a card with the same rows: how it works, what you gain, what you lose. The rows line up across cards, so you compare "lose" against "lose" by moving your eyes sideways. The pick is marked on its card, with the reason inside it.
  • Keyboard first, thumb friendly. 1 to 4 picks an option, ⏎ saves and moves to the next item, J/K moves, Z undoes. On a phone every control is 40px or more, and the queue and the item are two screens.
  • A visible reaction when you save. The chosen card turns green with a stamped check, the others fade, a toast names your answer and who gets it with a six-second Undo, the item slides into "Answered", the counter and bar move, and the page goes to the next item. When the pile is empty you get a summary of where each answer went.
  • A receipt, not a promise. Saving wakes no session today. So each answered item shows three steps: Saved, Picked up (the session read it), Filed (it reached its home and the item closed). On this mockup the last two steps are simulated with a timer.
  • Legibility. Atkinson Hyperlegible, a face built to keep look-alike letters apart. Body text at 16px, lines under 72 characters, urgency shown by shape and word as well as colour, both themes tuned separately.

Uniform, with one repo mark

Every repo gets the same page: same layout, same colours for state, same keys. Only one thing changes per repo: a 4px band and a small square in the repo's own colour, beside the repo's name. Try "Preview as" in the top bar to see it.

  • Why uniform: you move between nine pages. Red must mean "blocks work" and green must mean "saved" on all of them, or you stop trusting colour. Muscle memory for keys and button places only builds if they never move. One palette is checked for contrast once, in both themes.
  • Why still one mark: with two tabs open, you need to know at a glance which repo you are answering for. A band does that without touching anything you act on.
  • What a repo colour may never do: colour a button, an option, a state or text. Those stay on the shared palette.

What I would change in the template

  • Two new optional item fields: urgency (blocking, soon or whenever) and blocks (one sentence: what waits on the answer). Old items without them sort as "whenever", so no page breaks.
  • One field a session writes on the item: seen (time the session read the answer). The page shows it as "Picked up". "Filed" is the existing close. Sessions still never write answers/.
  • The skill: "Add an item" asks for urgency and blocks. "Read the answers" adds one write of seen.
  • The page: the layout above, the keys, the save reaction with Undo (Undo is the existing Clear), and the repo band read from meta/page (repo, hue).
  • Kept as is: the item numbers, "None of these" with a required note, the two-press "Accept every pick", the copy-as-one-message fallback when the store is unreachable.

Is it too late?

No. Every repo's page is the same fixed file, and all the content lives in the page's store. A new template can be republished to each existing link, so the links and the saved items stay. The data change above only adds fields, so older items still show. One check is still owed: that a republish to the same link keeps the store. I expect it does, but I have not measured it.
